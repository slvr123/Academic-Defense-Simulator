======================================================================
IMPORTANT: peak RPM is NOT attributable to the grounding-retry burst alone.
======================================================================
Isolating the retry-free window (turns 5-7, no retries): PanelistQuestion at
39.281s -> 46.797s -> 54.890s = ~7.5s between successive turns' question-gen
calls, each turn contributing 2 calls (question-gen + scoring) within
~1.0-1.1s of each other. That is a steady-state cadence of 2 calls per
~7.5s = ~16 calls/minute -- already at/over the 15 RPM ceiling with ZERO
grounding retries in play. Removing them from the peak-60s window (17 - 2 =
15) still lands exactly at the ceiling, not comfortably under it.

Root cause of the steady-state number: the old dual-sleep design (Day 4,
6570294) put one sleep right after question-gen and a second, separate one
at end-of-turn. That meant question-gen and scoring were never adjacent in
time -- question-gen's own 5s sleep separated them, producing two ~5s-apart
clusters per turn and an effective ~12 calls/minute. Removing the embedded
sleep collapsed that gap: question-gen and scoring now fire back-to-back
(only real API latency apart, ~1-1.1s), leaving only the turn-end 5s sleep
to pace the *whole* 2-call cluster -- which is why steady-state cadence rose
from ~12 to ~16 calls/minute even before any retry adds more.

LIMITER ADDED: sliding-window admission control in gemini_provider.py
(_throttle_for_rpm), scoped to every real network call (question-gen,
scoring, panel generation, report narrative, suggestions) -- not just the
grounding-retry loop, per the finding above. Module-level state keyed by
model name: sleeps zero while the rolling 60s window has room, sleeps only
the exact deficit once it's full. See gemini_provider.py for the full
docstring and tests/test_gemini_provider.py for unit coverage.

result that lands on the exact number already responsible for one real
failure.
