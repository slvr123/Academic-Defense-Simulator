## Decision 1 — Panel display: composition order, DA labeled, count captioned (LOCKED)

**Source finding this design responds to:** `compose_full_roster` (in `panel.py`)
appends Devil's Advocate outside the `panel_size` cap. A `panel_size=4` thesis
session seats 5; every session seats one more panelist than the selector value.
Effective ceilings: thesis/capstone 5 total, all `other/*` subtypes 4 total. There
is no DA-less session — the append is unconditional.

**Locked:**

1. Cards render `session.panel` in list order — which is composition order, which
   is round-robin speaking order (0.3b Decision 3, DA last). The card row itself is
   the speaking-order legend; no separate key.
2. A caption under the `panel_size` selector states the arithmetic explicitly, e.g.
   "4 domain panelists + Devil's Advocate = 5 total", updating with the selector
   and defense type. Without it, a user who picked 4 and sees 5 cards reads the
   surplus as a bug.
3. DA is differentiated by label/icon treatment, not a unique accent hue (see
   Decision 7). Its card sits last, matching when it actually speaks.

**In plain terms:** the code always adds the Devil's Advocate on top of whatever
panel size you pick — that was 0.3b's deliberate design (DA is an orchestration
feature, not a menu item), but nobody ever wrote down that the visible panel would
therefore always be one bigger than the number on the selector. Rather than change
the logic (out of scope, and the logic is right), the UI just says so plainly.
Decided by evidence (the `panel.py` source pull), not assumption.

## Decision 2 — Card states: waiting / speaking / completed (LOCKED)

Three states, derived entirely from existing session data — no new fields:

- **Speaking** — the panelist whose turn is active. Accent-bordered card
  (Decision 7 palette), status line "Speaking now".
- **Completed** — panelists with at least one turn in `session.turns` (matched via
  `panelist_archetype_key`) who are not currently speaking. Neutral card, status
  line shows question count ("2 questions asked").
- **Waiting** — no turns yet, not speaking. Muted card. DA's waiting card reads
  "Speaks last" instead of "Waiting" — it's true, it's flavor, and it telegraphs
  the session's shape.

The prototype's "Measured" temperament badge is **cut**. It corresponds to no
field on `Panelist`, and adding one means touching `PERSONA_GENERATION_PROMPT` and
the schema — business logic, out of 0.3d scope. Parked (see Parked items): a
one-word temperament could ride the existing persona-generation call for free
whenever personas are next touched for another reason.

**In plain terms:** each card just answers "has this panelist gone, are they going
now, or are they up later" — all computable from data the session already records
every turn. The badge the design tool invented got cut because it displayed a field
that doesn't exist; inventing the field is a different session's decision.

## Decision 3 — Speaking state: confirmed pure turn-indexed rotation (CONFIRMED)

**Source, verbatim from `engine.py`:**

```python
def select_active_panelist(session: DefenseSession, turn_num: int) -> Panelist:
    """Fixed round-robin: one turn per panelist per round, cycling `session.panel` in
    composition order. Devil's Advocate sits last in `session.panel` (see
    `panel.compose_full_roster`), so it naturally fires last in every round. No
    score-driven handoff, no floor retention — that is v0.3.x, gated on this being
    verified first (Decision 3)."""
    return session.panel[(turn_num - 1) % len(session.panel)]
```

**Confirmed:** rotation is a pure function of `turn_num` alone —
`session.panel[(turn_num - 1) % len(session.panel)]`. It has zero knowledge of
`_should_follow_up`'s branching. Whether turn N presses a prior weakness or opens a
fresh chunk, the panelist asking it is determined purely by `turn_num`'s position
in the roster cycle.

**The earlier working assumption in this doc — "same panelist retains the floor
during a follow-up" — was wrong**, corrected by source, not by design intent.
Recording that plainly rather than smoothing it over; the decided-by-reasoning /
decided-by-evidence distinction only means something if being wrong gets written
down when it happens.

**Consequence for card logic (good news):** simpler than planned. No conditional
on follow-up-vs-new-topic is needed — the speaking card for any turn is just
`session.panel[(turn_num - 1) % len(session.panel)]`, knowable even before that
turn's question is generated.

**Consequence for prompt correctness (a confirmed, live defect):**
`_should_follow_up` decides whether to press a prior weakness independently of who
is about to speak — per its own docstring, "any panelist may press a weakness
surfaced by whoever went before them." So `FOLLOWUP_SYSTEM_PROMPT` can render for
Panelist B with "You previously asked the candidate this question:
{previous_question}" where the question was actually asked by Panelist A. This is
the exact defect class already on record as a known learning
(`FOLLOWUP_SYSTEM_PROMPT` makes a false first-person claim when a different
panelist references a prior exchange) — previously a suspicion, now confirmed
live. `ConversationTurn.panelist_archetype_key` (0.3b) closed the *schema* gap that
made this undetectable; the *prompt* was never updated to branch on it.

**Out of scope for 0.3d** — a prompt-template change is business logic, and 0.3d's
own DoD is zero business-logic changes. Not fixed here. See "Carried defect" below
for the recommended fix and where it should land.

**In plain terms:** the code doesn't try to keep the same panelist on a follow-up
— it cycles through the roster every single turn, no matter what's being asked.
That's actually simpler for the cards to render. But it means the follow-up
prompt's "you previously asked this" line can end up in the mouth of a panelist
who didn't ask it — a real, confirmed inconsistency a careful reader would notice
in a transcript. Small, well-understood, not fixed today.

## Decision 4 — Dev-view contents and the difficulty-hiding extension (LOCKED)

Dev view is an `st.toggle` gating an expander, per the original ROADMAP spec.
Contents per turn:

1. Full `AnswerScore` — `clarity`, `depth`, `grounding`, `difficulty_delta`,
   `primary_gap` (original spec)
2. `grounding_retry_used` and `grounding_flagged` — new debug signals from the
   hardening session; same treatment as `AnswerScore`, resolving the open question
   flagged in the handoff (lean confirmed: yes, dev-view only)
3. `difficulty_level` of the current question — **new decision, extends the
   "never surfaced mid-session" rule**: displaying live difficulty leaks the same
   signal the rule protects. A candidate watching "Difficulty 4/5" tick upward is
   gaming the meter, not answering the panel. Difficulty appears in the main flow
   only after the session ends, inside the report's trajectory.
4. `PROMPT_VERSION` — static per session, but pinning it in dev-view lets any
   screenshot or live eval observation be cross-referenced against the exact
   template set that produced it.

Scores, deltas, gaps, flags, and difficulty never render anywhere in the main flow
mid-session. Non-negotiable, unchanged from the original rule — items 2 and 3 are
extensions of its coverage to signals that didn't exist when it was written.

**LOCKED (2026-07-13):** `grounding_reference` moves to dev-view, permanently —
same class as `AnswerScore`, `grounding_retry_used`, `grounding_flagged`,
`difficulty_level`, and `PROMPT_VERSION`. Found during live review of the
layout-revision build: the retrieved passage was rendering under every question
in the main flow, letting the candidate paraphrase the source instead of
answering from memory — the same open-book leak Decision 9 excludes from the
sidebar, just attached to every question instead of sitting there once. Sean
initially proposed leaving it visible during his own dev/testing and stripping
it before deployment; resolved in favor of moving it now — dev-view already
gives identical visibility on demand, so the deferred option bought no real
convenience, only a window (however short, and Decision 0 already defines
"deployment" as the friends beta, not a later date) where every tester would
see it. Never renders in the main flow again, on any turn, active or collapsed.

**In plain terms:** the dev toggle is a diagnostics drawer for eval runs — every
internal number about the current session, in one place, invisible unless
deliberately opened. The one genuinely new call here is hiding the difficulty
number too: the adaptive-difficulty mechanic only works as defense practice if the
candidate can't see the thermostat.

## Decision 5 — Aborted-state error path: fixed stage-aware copy, raw detail to logs (LOCKED)

**Source finding:** the aborted state is two bare `st.session_state` keys —
`stage = "aborted"` and `abort_message = str(exc)` — set at three call sites
(question-gen, scoring, report-build). `abort_message` is a stringified provider
exception; its content is not controlled and not confirmed presentable.

**Locked:** the same principle as Day 6 Decision 4 (the temp-path leak): user-facing
copy is fixed and written by us; raw exception text goes to logs only (already
captured by the permanent call-lifecycle logging) plus dev-view if the toggle is on.

Three fixed messages, keyed by which call site aborted:

- Question generation: "The panel's next question took longer than expected."
- Scoring: "Scoring your answer took longer than expected."
- Report build: "Building your report took longer than expected."

Each renders via `st.error()` with a single action: **Start a new session**. No
retry-in-place — the timeout guard exists precisely because the normal
retry-once path didn't catch whatever tripped it; a UI retry would paper over a
state the guard was built to surface.

Implementation note: the call-site key can ride the existing
`st.session_state` mechanism (e.g. an `abort_stage` label alongside
`abort_message`) — a Streamlit-layer change only, no business-logic module
touched. If the three call sites already pass a distinguishing `label` into
`_call_with_timeout`, reuse it.

**In plain terms:** when a call times out, the user sees a calm one-line
explanation of what stalled and a button to start over — never a Python exception.
The real error text isn't lost; it's in the logs where it belongs, and in dev-view
for anyone debugging. Same rule the project already applied once when a file path
leaked into an error message: users get prose, logs get truth.

## Decision 6 — Report view: layout over the real `DefenseReport` (LOCKED)

Renders from `session.report` at session end. Structure, top to bottom:

1. **Narrative** — front and center when present. When
   `narrative_fallback_used=True`: a plain "Narrative unavailable this session"
   line in the same slot. Never an empty gap, never an error tone — the numbers
   below are the report; the prose is garnish (0.3c Decision 4's framing).
2. **Difficulty trajectory** — rendered as the plain sequence
   (e.g. "2 → 3 → 3 → 4 → 3 → 2"). Six integers do not need a chart; a sparkline
   is decoration that costs rerun jank. Revisit only if v0.4+ sessions get long
   enough that the sequence stops being scannable.
3. **Session averages** — `overall_avg_clarity` / `depth` / `grounding`, one row
   of three metric displays.
4. **Per-panelist sections** — one per `panelist_sections` entry, composition
   order, reusing the card visual language from the live view: name, archetype,
   `turns_taken`, three averages, and the `primary_gaps` list verbatim.
5. **Pushback events** — a table: turn, difficulty movement
   (`difficulty_from → difficulty_to`), outcome. Outcome wording uses the enum
   values verbatim (`recovered` / `held` / `deteriorated`) — the report must stay
   hand-traceable to the transcript, and renaming buckets in the UI breaks that
   trace. Zero-escalation sessions render an explicit "No escalation moments this
   session" line — same transparency call 0.3c made on the metric itself (an
   empty list is a finding, not a gap to hide).

**In plain terms:** the report screen is a straight rendering of the report object
0.3c already built — nothing computed in the UI, nothing renamed, every number on
screen traceable to a field in the exported JSON. Where data is missing (no
narrative, no escalations) the screen says so in words instead of showing a hole,
because "nothing happened here" is information.

## Decision 8 — Exchange layout: hearing transcript, not chat (LOCKED)

**Amends Task 4 of the Code brief** — `st.chat_message` is retired for the
exchange. Chat bubbles lose speaker attribution as the session scrolls; a defense
is a hearing, not a DM thread.

**Locked structure:**

1. **Every exchange is a transcript block** with a permanent small-caps header:
   `TURN {n} — DR. {SURNAME} · {ARCHETYPE}`. Attribution is part of the block,
   not a floating avatar — it cannot scroll away from its content.
2. **The candidate's answer renders inside the same block** as a visually
   distinct inset (`YOU —` label, left-rule, recessed surface). One block = one
   complete exchange.
3. **Older turns collapse to their header line** (native `st.expander`,
   collapsed by default once the turn is scored) — a six-turn session reads as a
   scannable docket. The active turn is always full-size, accent-bordered, with
   the answer box inside it.
4. **The panelist avatar card row stays at the top of the session view,
   unchanged** (Decision 2 states and all). The transcript supplements the
   cards; it does not replace them.

**In plain terms:** every question-and-answer is filed like a court transcript
entry — numbered, named, and foldable — so you always know who asked what, no
matter how long the session gets. The cards up top show who's speaking *now*;
the transcript shows who said what *when*.

## Decision 9 — Case-file sidebar (LOCKED)

The native Streamlit (left) sidebar becomes the **case file** — session context
that currently has no home. A right rail was considered and rejected: Streamlit
has no native right sidebar, and faking one with columns buys rerun jank for
symmetry's sake.

**Contents (all already computed — zero new LLM calls, zero business-logic
changes):**

1. Document identity: filename, extracted domain and topic (same values as the
   intake form)
2. Document stats: page count, chunk count (known at ingestion)
3. Panel roster in miniature: every panelist with live state — keeps card states
   visible when scrolled deep into the transcript
4. Session progress: turn N of {MAX_TURNS}, defense type

**Explicitly excluded, with reasons:**

- **The retrieved excerpt each question grounds on** — showing it makes the
  defense open-book and guts the adaptive-difficulty mechanic. Real panels don't
  hand you the page they're quoting. (Sean's call, confirmed 2026-07-13.
  Revisitable post-deploy as an optional "open-book mode" if real beta feedback
  asks for it.)
- **Anything difficulty- or score-shaped** — the never-surfaced rule (Decision 4)
  does not bend for empty space.

**In plain terms:** the sidebar is everything a defense chair's folder would
hold about *your* submission — what you filed, how big it is, who's on your
panel, how far along you are — and nothing that would let you read the panel's
notes.

## Findings from live end-to-end testing (2026-07-13) — raised by Sean, discussed, sequencing not yet decided

Four observations from actually playing a full session, not design-table
speculation. None of these are 0.3d scope; recorded here so they aren't lost
before the next design day.

1. **Follow-ups can crowd out new topics across panelists.** Confirmed against
   `engine.py`: `select_active_panelist` rotates by pure turn number, completely
   decoupled from `_should_follow_up`'s branching — so a *different* panelist's
   turn can still be forced into pressing the *previous* panelist's identified
   weakness rather than introducing their own lane's material. Capped at
   `MAX_FOLLOW_UPS_PER_TOPIC = 2`, so not endless, but a real chunk of a 6-turn
   session. **This is Adaptive Turn Retention** (v0.3b backlog, gated on
   "round-robin verified clean first") — that gate is now satisfied. Sean
   independently rediscovered the exact problem this feature solves, twice.
   Candidate for the next real design day.
2. **No exposed difficulty adjuster; both failure modes confirmed present.**
   `difficulty_start` exists on `DefenseProfile` (default 2), never wired to a
   form control — same pre-exposure status `panel_size` had before 0.3a. Cheap
   to add, same shape of change. Sean confirms both: escalation climbs too
   fast, *and* even early questions feel sharp. The second is not obviously a
   `difficulty_start` problem — that only moves the entry point, it doesn't
   change how the model modulates tone once inside a difficulty level. Two
   distinct candidate causes, needing distinct evidence:
   (a) `SCORING_SYSTEM_PROMPT`'s escalation bias (Day 4: escalate on any
   flawed-but-engaged answer, ease only on a genuine non-answer) — governs
   *speed*, deliberate adversarial-practice intent, not obviously a bug;
   (b) `PANELIST_SYSTEM_PROMPT`/`FOLLOWUP_SYSTEM_PROMPT` may not meaningfully
   differentiate *tone* between difficulty 1–2 and 3–4 in practice, regardless
   of what the numeric target says — this would explain "sharp even early"
   independent of escalation speed entirely. (b) is untested; before touching
   either prompt, worth a small probe comparing real generated questions at
   difficulty 1 vs. 4 side by side — the kind of evidence v0.4's second-document
   eval is built for, not a same-night guess.
3. **Panel-size selector and Devil's Advocate — two different fixes on the
   table, not yet resolved which one.** Original proposal: keep `panel_size`
   meaning "domain count," translate at the UI layer only (selector shows the
