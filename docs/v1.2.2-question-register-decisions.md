## Decision 1 — Register applies to question prose only, never to `grounding_reference` (LOCKED)

This is the load-bearing decision of the slice. `grounding_reference` is matched
exact-then-fuzzy against the retrieved chunk, and `is_grounded()` already has a
documented systematic false-negative mode on assembled references. Any instruction
that loosens language near that field attacks the project's only cheap retrieval
signal.

**So the register block is scoped explicitly in the prompt text itself**, and a
second line is added beside the `grounding_reference` field instruction stating
that the register never applies to it.

*Provenance: Claude proposed the scoping and the second guard line; I locked both.*

**In plain terms:** the panelist may talk like a person, but the quoted bit it
points at stays word-for-word from the document.

## Decision 2 — Exact block wording (LOCKED — verbatim, no paraphrase)

Appended as its own labeled section in the question-generation template. The
wording *is* the decision; Code does not reword it.

```
DELIVERY REGISTER:
Ask the question the way an examiner says it out loud, not the way a form field reads.
- Open with one short lead-in clause naming the specific claim, number, or decision from the excerpt you are pressing on. Then ask the question.
- Use contractions. Vary sentence length — put a short sentence next to a longer one.
- You may hedge the lead-in ("I'm not sure I follow the reasoning here."). Never hedge the question itself — ask it directly.
- Do not use filler or disfluency tokens ("um", "uh", "well,"), humor, sarcasm, idioms, or slang.
- Do not cite a page, figure, or table number unless that number appears verbatim in the excerpt.
- When referring to the document's content, use the document's own terminology. Do not substitute synonyms for its terms.
- The register above governs your question text only. It never applies to grounding_reference.
```

And beside the existing `grounding_reference` field instruction:

```
grounding_reference must remain a verbatim span copied from the excerpt. The delivery register does not apply to this field.
```

**Two bullets are doing defensive work and must not be trimmed as redundant.**
The no-page-numbers line prevents fabricated citations, since chunks don't
reliably carry page numbers and an invented "page 12" is a fabrication the
grounding check won't catch. The document's-own-terminology line is the direct
counter to the rejected "unexpected word alternatives" instruction — it points
the model at the source vocabulary rather than away from it, which is what
protects the fuzzy match.

*Provenance: Claude drafted the block from the Decision 6 keeper list and added
the two defensive bullets; I locked it verbatim.*

**In plain terms:** four lines make it sound human, three lines stop it inventing
citations, drifting off the document's words, or loosening the quoted reference.

## Decision 3 — Probe design: deterministic A/B on the same chunks (LOCKED)

Retrieval per archetype is a fixed focus string → fixed embedding → fixed
ranking → **identical chunk sequence per document, every session.** That standing
property, normally a limitation, makes this a genuinely clean A/B: the same chunks
come back before and after, so any change in grounding rate is attributable to
phrasing alone.

**Baseline is captured in this session, not read from history.** Prior grounding
evidence was gathered under different conditions and is not comparable.

- **Document:** DAZSMA.
- **Arms:** BEFORE (current template, unmodified) and AFTER (register block added).
- **Matrix:** 3 archetypes × 3 difficulty levels (1, 3, 5) = 9 questions per arm,
  18 generation calls total. Archetypes: `methodology_expert`,
  `statistical_analysis_reviewer`, `technical_implementation_reviewer` —
  the three carrying the most existing lane evidence.
- **Recorded per row:** archetype, difficulty, chunk index, question text,
  `grounding_reference`, `is_grounded()` result, flagged/retry status.

**Metrics, decided before seeing the data:**

1. **Grounding rate** — AFTER must not be *lower* than BEFORE.
2. **Flagged-turn count** — AFTER must not be higher than BEFORE.
3. **Lane adherence** — judged by me from a rendered `in_lane [ ]` sheet in the
   `v1_1a-judging-aid.md` format, both arms, unlabeled as to arm.
4. **Fabricated locators** — zero page/figure/table numbers in AFTER that do not
   appear in the retrieved chunk. Any hit is an automatic fail.

*Provenance: Claude proposed the determinism-exploiting A/B, the matrix, and the
four metrics; I locked them.*

**In plain terms:** ask the same nine questions twice, once with the new wording
and once without, on identical source passages — then check nothing got worse.

## Decision 4 — Pre-committed decision rule (LOCKED, all branches enumerated)

Decided now, before any output exists.

- **Grounding rate holds AND flagged count holds AND lanes hold AND zero
  fabricated locators** → **ship.** Bump `PROMPT_VERSION`, commit.
- **Grounding rate drops OR flagged count rises** → **revert the block entirely.**
  Not tuned, not narrowed — reverted. This was the stopping rule agreed in
  Decision 6 and it closes the question either way.
- **Grounding holds but lane adherence degrades** → **revert.** v1.1a lane wording
  carries more evidence than this slice does, and I am not trading it for register.
- **Any fabricated locator appears** → **revert**, and record it as a finding about
  the model rather than about the wording.
- **Something none of these describe** (e.g. output changes in a way that is
  neither better nor worse but clearly different, or the two arms are not
  comparable because retrieval did not in fact repeat) → **stop and bring it back
  to design.** Do not pick a branch above by default.

Halt branch included on purpose, per the codified lesson from the sleep-fix rule
failure.

**In plain terms:** one run decides it. If anything measurable got worse, the
wording comes out and we move on to the retrieval eval — no tuning loop.
