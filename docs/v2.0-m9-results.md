## 1. What changed, and the evidence

### L2 - routing by role

A panelist who isn't allowed the literature review now never retrieves a chunk M7 tagged as literature review, fully or partly.
`backend/routing.py` wraps whichever retriever the defense uses (M6's seeded `NearTieRetriever`) and adds the blocked chunks to `exclude_indices`.
The near-tie band is then formed only among chunks the panelist may draw.
`academic_defense_simulator/` is unchanged.
`backend/defense.py` already knows the asking panelist when it builds the retriever, so nothing had to be derived from the query string.

`ADS_LIT_REVIEW_ACCESS` has two values.
`literature` lets only the Literature & Theory Specialist draw tagged chunks.
`none` bars everyone, and it is the default after L5.
Follow-ups reuse the panelist's own chunk, and Devil's Advocate contests a chunk already asked about, so neither path retrieves.
`/start` now also refuses, with a 422, a panel whose barred panelists outnumber the document's untagged chunks; without that check, v1 would raise "document may be exhausted" mid-defense.
The defense setup log line gains `lit_review_chunks=` and `lit_review_access=`.

## 3. Concepts map

- **Routing data by role** (`backend/routing.py:41`, used at `backend/defense.py:245`).
  Each panelist gets the list of chunks it may not retrieve, worked out from the tags ingest already stores, so who can see what is decided by data rather than by asking the model to behave.
  The same document serves different panelists different slices of itself, just as a real panel splits a thesis by expertise.
- **Why a deterministic filter beats prompt wording here** (`backend/routing.py:67` against `backend/engine_adapter.py:89`).
  Two note wordings took the error rate from 55% to about 30% and then stalled, because a prompt only nudges a model that still has the cited system in front of it.
  A filter removes the case: a panelist that never sees the passage can't presume the candidate built what it describes, and the L4 tables show that holds exactly, not on average.
- **An independent judge, and why the author shouldn't grade their own fix** (`scripts/probe_attribution_r3.py:71`, `scripts/score_attribution_judging_r3.py`).
  Whoever wrote the note knows what it was meant to prevent and reads questions charitably toward it; my quick scan and Claude's careful check also disagreed by up to 4 items per arm.
  A fresh agent on another model, seeing only the rubric and the sheet, can't lean either way, and the verdict was fixed before its labels existed.
- **Composing two retrievers** (`backend/routing.py:50`, `backend/retrieval_sampling.py:45`).
  `RoutedRetriever` has the same call shape as the retriever it wraps and changes only one argument, the excluded set, so the near-tie sampler underneath works unchanged on a smaller pool.
  Each piece can be tested alone, and the composition is tested once: the routed pick must equal the sampler's pick over the union of exclusions.
