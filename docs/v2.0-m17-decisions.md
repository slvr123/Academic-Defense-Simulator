## Decision 1: Own key, entered once, held only in memory on the server

**Entry (frontend).**
- A small "Use your own Gemini key" control next to the quota line on the start screen. It's collapsed by default.
- When today's free defenses are used up, it becomes the main suggestion: "Today's free defenses are used. Use your own free Gemini key to keep practicing."
- Opening it shows:
  - a password-type input;
  - a "Use this key" button;
  - one help line: "Free, about 60 seconds: aistudio.google.com/apikey. Sign in with Google, then Create API key. No card needed." (Link it.)
- **The frontend never stores the key:** no `localStorage`, no `sessionStorage`, nothing in the URL. It's sent once (Decision 2) and then dropped from page state.

**Validation.**
- A new `POST /keys/check` takes the key in an `X-Gemini-Key` header. It makes one `models.list` call with it (no generation) and returns `{ok: true}` or a coded `invalid_key`. Nothing is stored.
- **The error copy:** "Google rejected this key. Check for missing characters, or create a fresh one."

**Server side.**
- The upload (`POST /sessions`) carries the key in the same `X-Gemini-Key` header, never as a form field.
- The backend validates it again with `models.list`, then holds it **on the in-memory session only**, as a `SecretStr`, and builds that defense's providers with it.
- The key dies with the session: on End session, on expiry, or on restart. A page refresh mid-defense still works, because the key is already on the server.
- **CORS:** allow the `X-Gemini-Key` request header.

**Never:**
- logged, including by the access log or error handlers;
- returned by any endpoint, including `/current`, the report and the transcript;
- written to the database;
- shown in the UI after entry.

## Decision 2: Own-key defenses don't touch the free quota

- **No daily cap and no per-IP limit.** It's the visitor's own Google quota. Own-key defenses skip `quota.reserve` entirely, so they can't use up or refund free slots.
- **Server limits still apply:** upload size and page caps, and the single ingest queue.
- **The quota line reads "Using your own key"** instead of "n of 10 left".
- **Voice (MiMo) stays on the server's key,** with its existing limits.

## Decision 3: The suggestions trap

Google reportedly limits `gemini-2.5-flash` to projects that used it before 2026-09-18, and most visitors will create fresh keys.
- For own-key defenses, wrap the suggestions provider on the v2 side.
- Try `gemini-2.5-flash`. If it fails with a "model not available" or permission error (not a 503), retry that call once on the defense model, `gemini-3.1-flash-lite`.
- Log only the fallback reason, never the key.
- A 503 keeps today's behavior.
- Server-key defenses are **unchanged.**
- **No v1 change.**

## Decision 4: Errors mid-defense

- **Revoked key or auth failure:** a coded `own_key_rejected`, with the copy "Your Gemini key was rejected partway through. Paste a fresh key to start a new defense."
- **The visitor's key hits its own rate limit:** a coded `own_key_limited`, with the copy "Your Gemini key hit its own limit. Wait a minute and try again, or come back after midnight Pacific."
- **These never blame the panel or Gemini overload.** `panel_busy` keeps its meaning.

## Decision 5: A "How it works" section

- **Where:** on the start screen, below the two columns, with a "How it works" link in the header that scrolls to it. It's **not shown during a defense.** Same Red Pen and Chamber tokens, and it stays readable at phone width.
- **Copy:** below. It's Sean's draft via Claude. Code may tighten wording but not add claims. **Every claim must be backed by the code or a results doc.** List each claim with its evidence in the results doc, and cut anything that isn't backed.

> **How it works**
>
> **RAG, turned around.** Most RAG apps search your documents to answer your questions. This one searches your paper to question *you*. Each panelist looks for something worth challenging (a claim, a number, a method choice) and asks about it. After you answer, you see the exact passage and page it came from.
>
> **A panel, not a chatbot.** Each panelist has a lane: problem and objectives, methodology, results. Each opens in its own chapter, so the defense walks through your paper the way a real one does. A Terror Panelist speaks last and goes after your earlier answers.
>
> **It adapts, quietly.** Every answer is scored against a rubric you don't see. Weak answers draw follow-ups, and the questions get harder as you do well. Scores appear only in the debrief, with suggestions grounded in the same passages.
>
> **Measured, not guessed.** Early panelists quizzed students on other authors' studies cited in their literature review, as if that work were theirs. That was measured, independently judged, and fixed by keeping those passages away from the panel. A chapter filter took opening questions that start in the right chapter from 5 of 10 to 10 of 10 on our eval.
>
> Built by Sean Silver Allata with FastAPI, React and Gemini, with retrieval in plain numpy. [Source on GitHub](https://github.com/slvr123/Academic-Defense-Simulator)

## Tests and checks

- **Backend tests:**
  - `/keys/check` accepts and rejects, using a fake client;
  - the key is held on the session and used to build the providers, with a sentinel key asserted to reach the client config;
  - an own-key start never calls `quota.reserve`;
  - the suggestions fallback fires on "model not available" but not on a 503;
  - both new error codes.
- **Leak test:** drive a full own-key defense with a sentinel key through the fake provider. Capture every log line, every response body (including `/current`, the report and errors) and the settings dump. Show `grep` finding **0** occurrences of the sentinel.
- **Frontend tests:**
  - the control is collapsed by default and becomes the main suggestion when the quota is used up;
  - the key isn't in any storage after entry;
  - the quota line reads "Using your own key";
  - the "How it works" section and header link appear on the start screen, and are hidden during a defense.
- **One live check, pre-authorized:**
  - One own-key defense against the deployed backend, using the dev-project key from `backend/.env`. Read it inside the script; never print or paste it.
  - Show that the free quota count didn't change before and after, and whether suggestions came from `gemini-2.5-flash` or the fallback.
  - It uses no free daily slot.
- **Capture:** retake `start-desktop.png` and `session-phone.png` if the start screen or header changed visibly. Same method as M13.
