## 1. Stop point A - the daily cap

### How many closing calls a session can make

A closing line happens on every hand-off: the next question comes from a different panelist than the one just answered (Decision 1).
v1's `select_active_panelist` (`academic_defense_simulator/engine.py:63-85`) bounds that:
- a follow-up keeps the same panelist;
- a new topic goes only to a domain panelist who hasn't opened one yet (`domain_panelists[len(session.used_chunk_indices) % len(domain_panelists)]`, after the M12 opening order);
- the Terror Panelist speaks once, after every domain panelist (`engine.py:80-82`);
- `session_is_complete` (`engine.py:199-215`) ends the session after the Terror Panelist's chain: "No second round; no loop-back to domain panelists after DA."

So each panelist holds the floor in one unbroken block, and a full 4-seat panel hands off **3 times**: domain to domain twice, then to the Terror Panelist.
The loop's own ceiling, every turn after the first a hand-off, is `turn_cap(4) - 1 = 11`, but the code above never reaches it.

### Before and after

The cap is `floor(RPD / worst-case calls per session) - margin` for each model, smallest wins (`backend/quota.py`).
Computed with the real `plan_quota` and default settings, adding the closing calls on the defense model as the decision says:

```
BEFORE  panel size 4 | turns 12
    gemini-2.5-flash calls/session 1 rpd 20 -> sessions 20
    gemini-3.1-flash-lite calls/session 40 rpd 500 -> sessions 12
    binding: gemini-3.1-flash-lite | cap 11 (margin 1 )
AFTER   +3 closing calls (one closing per hand-off between topic blocks: panel_size - 1)
    gemini-2.5-flash calls/session 1 rpd 20 -> sessions 20
    gemini-3.1-flash-lite calls/session 43 rpd 500 -> sessions 11
    binding: gemini-3.1-flash-lite | cap 10
AFTER   +11 closing calls (loop bound: every turn after the first a hand-off, turn_cap - 1)
    gemini-2.5-flash calls/session 1 rpd 20 -> sessions 20
    gemini-3.1-flash-lite calls/session 51 rpd 500 -> sessions 9
    binding: gemini-3.1-flash-lite | cap 8
margin 0 with +3: 11
```

| Count used for closing calls | flash-lite calls per session | Sessions per day | Daily cap |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| none (before M15) | 40 | 12 | **11** |
| 3, one per hand-off (the real bound) | 43 | 11 | **10** |
| 11, the turn loop's ceiling | 51 | 9 | **8** |
| 3, with `quota_margin` 0 instead of 1 | 43 | 11 | **11** |

### My decision

I chose option 1: count exactly 3 closing calls per session in `quota.py`, for a daily cap of 10, and build M15 as written.
Claude recommended it because 3 is the exact bound for the engine as it stands.

## 2. What was built

**Backend (v2 side only; `git diff --stat main -- academic_defense_simulator` is empty).**
- `backend/closing.py`: the closing call on its own thread (`ClosingCall`), started before the next question is generated, and `take()` waits at most 1 s once the question is ready.
  `problem()` rejects an empty line, more than 30 words, a "?" or more than two sentences.
  `log_closing` logs counts and timings only, never the line, the answer or a passage.
- `backend/prompts/closing_prompts.py`: the closing prompt (section 3).
- `backend/defense.py`: `answer()` picks the next speaker with v1's `select_active_panelist` once, starts the closing call when that speaker differs from the one just answered, then generates the question for that same speaker, so the hand-off check and the question can't disagree.
  Since the amendment, it also passes the session's earlier closing lines, in order, into the prompt.
- `backend/quota.py`: `CLOSING_CALLS_PER_SESSION = MAX_PANEL_SIZE - 1`, counted only when `ADS_CLOSING_LINES=llm`.
- API: an optional `closing` (panelist with display title, plus the line) on the answer response, on `/current` (the line said just before the current question) and on each transcript turn.
  `/tts` takes `closing: true` to speak the closing line said after a turn, so the server still speaks only text it generated.
- `ADS_CLOSING_LINES`: `llm` (default), `fixed` or `off`.

**Frontend.**
- On a hand-off the outgoing card stays lit with status "Closing" while its line shows alone, in the question's treatment but smaller and quieter, with no answer box.
- The line holds for `CLOSING_HOLD_MS = 1600`, or until a click or key press, or, with voice on, until the line has been spoken; then the next card lights and its question appears.
- The next question's voice is prefetched but held during the closing line.
- The transcript drawer lists "Dr. X closes: ..." after the turn it followed.
- The demo replay renders a recorded closing line with the same pacing.
- The debrief report is unchanged.

**Voice was implemented, not skipped:** the `/tts` proxy took the closing line cleanly as `closing: true` on the outgoing turn, a small change.

**Flagged, reduced motion:** the decision says the line is "shown instantly, with no timed hold" under reduced motion.
With voice off, Claude shows the closing line at once, inline above the next question, so nothing waits.
With voice on, the closing line is still spoken before the question, because the order of speech isn't motion.

## 3. The closing prompt, as shipped

```
You are Dr. {panelist_name}, the {archetype_title} on a {defense_type} defense panel.
{persona_framing}

The defense is on: {topic}

You have just finished your line of questioning. A colleague on the panel takes over next. Say one brief closing line, out loud, before you hand over.

Your last question to the candidate was:
"{question}"

The candidate answered:
"{answer}"
{earlier_lines}
Rules for your closing line:
1. Speak as yourself, closing your own line of questioning before a colleague takes over.
2. Do not evaluate the answer. You may say a plain 'thank you', but never thank them for clarifying, explaining, justifying or addressing anything, and never mention concerns, reservations, doubts or how convincing it was. Close by saying your questions on this topic are done, in your own words; vary the phrasing naturally.
3. You may name the topic you covered, such as "your sampling" or "the scope you set", but never its quality.
4. Do not ask anything, and do not name or introduce the next panelist.
5. One or two short sentences, in a natural spoken register, at most 30 words in all.

Return only the closing line in the line field.
```

`{earlier_lines}` is empty on a session's first hand-off.
From the second hand-off on, it is:

```

Earlier in this defense, these closing lines were already said, in this order:
- "<first closing line, verbatim>"
- "<second closing line, verbatim>"
Do not begin your closing line with the same opening words as any of them.
```

Fallback lines count as said and are listed too.

The rule 2 that E1 ran on, before the rerun:

```
2. Do not evaluate the answer in any way. No praise and no criticism. Never use words like "good", "great", "excellent", "strong", "impressive", "weak", "unconvincing", "concerning" or "disappointing", and do not imply the answer satisfied or worried you. The candidate's scores stay hidden until the end of the defense.
```

## 7. The one allowed rerun - a controlled replay

### Why a replay instead of a fresh E1

A faithful rerun is 10 more defenses and about 200 calls.
That would take E1 to 20 defenses against a budget of 12, and the 204 calls already spent on the shared production key that day put the live site at risk of hitting the daily limit before the reset.
So I chose a controlled replay: `scripts/replay_closing_lines.py` makes the closing call again on the same 30 recorded hand-offs, with the same question and the same answer, so the only thing that changes is the prompt.
This replay is the **one allowed rerun** under Decision 6.

**Personas were regenerated.** E1 didn't save them, so each replayed session got a fresh panel (one persona call), and the outgoing panelist is that panel's member with the recorded archetype.
The recorded band and score carry over unchanged.

I replaced rule 2 with my own wording (section 3): a plain "thank you" is allowed, but never thanks for clarifying, explaining, justifying or addressing anything, never concerns, reservations, doubts or how convincing it was, and a close in the panelist's own words, with varied phrasing.

The ship rule was fixed before the run: at least 27 of 30 neutral, and at most 2 non-neutral lines matching their band.
I also asked for a repetitiveness count: how many of the 30 lines share their first four words.

## 8. Amendment - earlier lines in the prompt, tested as a chained replay

### What I authorised, and why

I authorised one more prompt revision beyond the doc's single rerun, because the replay fixed neutrality but left every panel sounding like one template.

### How it was tested

A chained replay over the 10 recorded sessions, in order, so each call sees the lines its session had already said (`scripts/replay_closing_lines.py --chained`, output `evals/v2.0-m15-closing-lines-chained.json`).
As in the replay, the questions and answers are E1's, and the personas were regenerated.
I ran it alone, because a low-memory kill had stopped the run before.

Rules, fixed before running:
- **Neutrality:** as before, at least 27 of 30 neutral, and at most 2 non-neutral matching their band.
- **Variety:** passes if, in at least 9 of 10 sessions, no two model-written closing lines share their first three words (case and punctuation ignored).
- **Outcomes:** both pass, ship `llm`. Neutrality passes but variety fails, ship `llm` and record variety as a known limit. Neutrality fails, ship `fixed`.

**Running it.** Gemini was overloaded when it started: 32 × `503 UNAVAILABLE` and 4 × `504 DEADLINE_EXCEEDED` in the first attempt, with every line a fallback.
Claude stopped that attempt, which saved nothing.
Decision 6 says to wait out 503s, so the script now waits 60 s and makes a call again when it errored or timed out (an invalid line stands as said), and the same for a persona call that fell back to the static panel.
A drop in this machine's network (`getaddrinfo failed`) crashed a second attempt, so a network error is waited out too.
The script now saves after every hand-off, with each session's regenerated panel, so it resumes with the same personas.
The recorded run took two windows and needed 7 retries in all; every one of the 30 lines is a model line.
