## What exists, and what changes

Since v0.3, when a new panelist takes over after an answer, the incoming panelist opens with one AI-written acknowledgment of that answer, merged into the question text (for example, "Fair enough on the usability metrics, but…").

**That stays exactly as it is.** M15 adds the other half of a real hand-off: **the outgoing panelist closes** before the next one speaks.

> **Dr. Lim** (Methodology Expert): "Alright, that's what I wanted to understand about your sampling. I'll leave it there."
> **Dr. Reyes** (Results & Conclusions): "Fair enough on the sampling, but…"

## Decision 1: When a closing line happens

- **When:** on every **hand-off**, meaning the next question comes from a different panelist than the one the candidate just answered. That includes hand-offs to and from the Terror Panelist.
- **Not:** on follow-ups (same panelist continues), on the first question, or at session end (the report screen is its own transition).
- Use the same "who speaks next" decision the engine already makes (`select_active_panelist` after the answer is recorded). Don't add a second rule that could disagree with it.

## Decision 2: The closing line is written by the outgoing panelist, in its own call (v2 side only)

**Zero v1 changes in M15.**
- The prompt is **new**, so it doesn't touch any locked prompt. It lives on the v2 side, in `backend/prompts/closing_prompts.py`, kept out of Python logic per the architecture rules.
- It's called through the existing provider abstraction, on the same defense model.

**Output schema:** a Pydantic model `ClosingLine { line: str }`. Validate it as **one or two sentences, at most 30 words.** Anything longer counts as a failure (Decision 4).

**What the prompt gives the model:**
- the outgoing panelist's name, persona framing and archetype title, so the voice matches;
- the defense type and topic;
- the question they asked and the candidate's answer.

**The rules the prompt states:**
1. Speak as this panelist, closing your line of questioning before a colleague takes over.
2. **Don't evaluate the answer.** No praise, no criticism, and no words like "good", "excellent", "weak", "unconvincing" or "concerning". The score is never revealed mid-session, and the incoming panelist already reacts to the content.
3. You may name the **topic** you covered ("your sampling", "the scope you set"), never its quality.
4. Don't ask anything, and don't introduce the next panelist by name. The UI shows who's next.
5. One or two short sentences, spoken register.

The prompt's final wording is Code's to draft from these rules. **Quote it in full in the results doc** for Sean's review.

**Concurrency:** start the closing call **at the same time** as the next question's generation, once the next speaker is known. That way a hand-off adds no waiting.
- Measure the added latency on hand-off turns: median over 10 or more hand-offs, with real timings shown.
- If it's more than 0.5 s median, report it with the cause.

## Decision 3: Quota

Every hand-off is one more call, about 3–4 per session. The daily cap comes from each model's RPD divided by worst-case calls per session (`backend/quota.py`, which reads v1's `call_budget`).
- Add the closing calls to the plan **on the v2 side**, inside `quota.py`'s calculation. Don't edit v1's `call_budget`.
- Show the daily cap and the binding model before and after.
- **STOP POINT A:** the daily cap goes down. Report the before and after numbers, and Sean decides.

## Decision 4: Failure never blocks a turn

If the closing call fails (a 503 streak, a timeout, an invalid or too-long output), **the next question still arrives on time.** The hand-off then uses a fixed neutral fallback such as "Thank you. That's all from me for now.", attributed to the outgoing panelist.
- **Timeout:** the closing call may not hold up the next question. If it hasn't returned by the time the question is ready, plus 1 s, use the fallback.
- **The fallback is logged** with the reason (no answer or passage text in the log).
- **Test:** a forced failure still delivers the next question, with the fallback line.

## Decision 5: API and UI

**API.**
- `AnswerResult` gains an optional `closing` field: the panelist's archetype key, name and title, plus the line. Use the display title, so "Terror Panelist" applies.
- `/current` and the transcript carry closing lines wherever they carry questions, so a resync doesn't lose them.
- Regenerate the frontend API types (`gen:api`).

**UI.**
- On a hand-off, the outgoing panelist's card **stays lit** while their closing line appears. It's shown as a spoken line, in the same treatment as a question but visibly shorter and quieter, with no answer box.
- Then the next panelist's card lights and their question appears.
- **Pacing:** the closing line stays alone for about 1.6 s, or until the user clicks or presses a key, whichever comes first. Under reduced motion it's shown instantly, with no timed hold.
- **GSAP:** the M11.1 lesson applies. Anything that must end up visible gets an explicit end state.
- **Transcript drawer:** closing lines appear in order, attributed to their panelist.
- **Voice:** when voice is on, speak the closing line in the outgoing panelist's voice before the next question.
  - If the `/tts` proxy can't take it cleanly (it takes a session and turn, never raw text), add the closing line as a part of the outgoing turn.
  - If that's more than a small change, **skip voice for closing lines**, say so in the results doc, and keep going.
- **The debrief report doesn't change.**

## Decision 6: Measurement, with the rule written before the data

**E1: is the closing line neutral?**
- Run AniCheck defenses until there are **at least 30 hand-offs.** Use the thesis preset, with real answers in a mix of strong and weak.
  - Claude (in Code) writes the answers from the paper, as M11 and M14 did.
  - Aim for a third strong (12+ out of 15), a third mid, and a third weak (6 or less).
- List all 30+ closing lines **verbatim** in the results doc, each with:
  - the score band of the answer it follows;
  - Code's classification (**neutral**, **positive** or **negative**) with a one-line reason.
- **Rule:**
  - **Ship** if at least 90% are neutral **and** no more than 2 non-neutral lines match the band they follow (positive after strong, or negative after weak).
  - **Otherwise:** allow **one** revision of the closing prompt and rerun E1 once. If it still fails, ship with the closing call off: fixed neutral lines only, behind the flag below. Report the result honestly, and decide next steps in chat.
- **Spend:** up to 12 defenses (the daily slots allow it across two days) on `gemini-3.1-flash-lite`. Wait out 503s; never switch models.

**E2: does it read naturally?** The results doc includes 3 complete hand-offs verbatim: question, answer summary, closing line, then the incoming opener. Sean reads them in chat. This isn't a merge gate.

**Flag:** `ADS_CLOSING_LINES` takes `llm`, `fixed` or `off`. The default is `llm` if E1 passes, and `fixed` otherwise. It can be switched on Render with no deploy.
