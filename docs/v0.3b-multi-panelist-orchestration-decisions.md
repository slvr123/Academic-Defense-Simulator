# v0.3b — Multi-Panelist Orchestration: Design Decisions

**Status:** LOCKED — 2026-07-10
**Scope:** Cross-panelist context, Devil's Advocate design, turn-taking, call budget, Miss 2 fold-ins, engine/UI loop extraction.
**Supersedes:** Open questions carried from the v0.3a handoff (2026-07-09).

This document doubles as the project's agent-architecture writeup. It records not just what was decided, but why the alternatives lose.

---

## Context

The Academic Defense Simulator runs a four-panelist defense panel over a candidate's document. Through v0.3a, panelists operate turn-by-turn with no knowledge of each other's exchanges. v0.3b introduces multi-panelist orchestration: panelists must build on — and in one case, attack — what has already been said, without degrading persona distinctness or document grounding.

Two constraints shape every decision below:

1. **Pattern-1 dilution** (documented failure mode): when a panelist's context is dominated by other voices, personas converge and questions repeat. The chunk template has excluded panelist identity since Day 1 for this reason.
2. **Standing constraint 2:** per-session LLM call budget must be stated explicitly; deviations require justification against the stated figure.

---

## Decision 1 — Cross-panelist context: structured digest (LOCKED)

**Decision:** Each panelist's context includes a **structured digest** of all prior turns, rendered from session state. No transcript. No rolling summary.

**Digest schema (locked):**

```
per prior turn:
  panelist_id
  persona_name
  question          # verbatim
  answer_summary    # 1–2 sentences of what the candidate claimed,
                    # emitted by the existing scoring call
  primary_gap
  score
```

Rendered chronologically into the active panelist's context. Capped at the last N turns; **N is an implementation decision, not a design decision** — pick it when session-length behavior is observable.

**Schema amendment vs. ROADMAP's original lean:** `answer_summary` is added to the **existing scoring schema**. The scorer already reads the full answer to produce `primary_gap`; emitting a claim summary is marginal tokens on a call already being made. Zero new LLM calls. Without this field the digest cannot support the Devil's Advocate (Decision 2) and the architecture would eventually be forced back toward transcripts.

**Why the alternatives lose:**

- **Full transcript** — maximizes Pattern-1 dilution (by turn 6, the active panelist's context is dominated by other voices); floods context with candidate-authored non-document text, scaling up the exact input that produced Miss 2; token cost grows triangularly across the session, paid on both calls per turn; and if dilution appears in probes, remediation is building the digest anyway plus re-validation.
- **Rolling summary** — adds one new LLM call per turn (violates the call budget) and introduces summary drift as a new failure surface.
- **Digest failure mode is graceful:** if probes show the digest is *insufficient* (repetitive or shallow cross-references), the fix is adding a field. Dilution's failure mode is subtle persona flattening that must be detected and then re-architected out.

## Decision 2 — Devil's Advocate: digest-driven, grounded, claims-only (LOCKED)

**Decision:** DA selects the **strongest / highest-scored prior claim** from the digest's `answer_summary` fields and contests it, grounded in the source document.

**Constraints locked into the persona template and pipeline:**

1. **DA passes through `is_grounded()` like every other panelist.** No adversarial exemption.
2. **DA contests claims, never the candidate personally.** One explicit sentence in the persona template.
3. **DA at difficulty 4–5 is a named probe case for the pre-deploy hardening session.** Adversarial reframing is where the difficulty-4 fabrication tension concentrates; the hardening session's validation must deliberately probe DA at forced difficulty 5. This is recorded here so the hardening session inherits it as a requirement, not a suggestion.

**Why free transcript-attack loses:** unbounded target selection is unverifiable (no probe can have an expected outcome); it hands the highest-fabrication-risk persona the largest supply of non-document text; and it silently reopens Decision 1 (either everyone gets the transcript, or DA gets a special context path that splits testing in two).

---

## Decision 3 — Turn-taking: round-robin, DA last (LOCKED)

**Decision:** Fixed round-robin rotation. **Devil's Advocate sits last in the round**, so the digest is maximally populated before it fires.

Score-driven handoff remains parked at v0.3.x, contingent on round-robin being verified first. Its concrete mechanism is specified in the v0.3.x backlog below (**Adaptive turn retention**) — the old "score-driven handoff" placeholder is superseded by that sharper design.

---

## Decision 4 — Per-session LLM call budget (DECLARED)

- **2 calls per turn:** 1 generation + 1 scoring. Active panelist only.
- **Digest rendering: 0 calls** (session state → template string).
- **Session of T turns = 2T calls total.**

Any v0.3.x proposal (score-driven handoff, rolling summaries, retry loops from the hardening session's escalation decision) must justify its delta against **2T**.

## Decision 6 — Engine/UI loop extraction: do it now (LOCKED)

**Decision:** The turn loop moves into an **engine module**. `main.py` and `streamlit_app.py` become thin drivers.

**Rationale beyond "we're restructuring the loop anyway":**

- The parked Streamlit hang cannot currently be bisected — the loop is coupled to both orchestration logic and Streamlit's execution model, so "is the hang ours or Streamlit's" is unanswerable. A clean engine/UI boundary gives the hang investigation a bisection point for free: if the engine runs clean under the CLI driver (already evidenced by the v0.3a 6-turn CLI session), the hang is isolated to the Streamlit layer.
- FastAPI migration becomes a third driver instead of a rewrite.

## v0.3.x backlog — designed, NOT built in 0.3b

### Adaptive turn retention (supersedes the "score-driven handoff" placeholder)

**Gate:** round-robin (Decision 3) verified running clean first. Do not build on an unverified rotation.

**Idea:** a panelist who hits a weak/evasive answer retains the floor for one or more consecutive follow-ups — chasing the gap down immediately, like a real defense — instead of yielding to the next panelist and only returning a full round later. This is the actual mechanism the vague "score-driven handoff" note was gesturing at; note it is *floor-retention*, not merely *reordering* the rotation.

**Design captured (to be locked at its own design day):**

1. **Trigger must be a hard, machine-checkable rule** — most naturally `score < threshold X` on the primary_gap dimension. Score is already in the digest schema, so no new plumbing. No "urgency" heuristic that can't be probed.
2. **Depth cap is mandatory** — e.g. max 2 consecutive follow-ups per panelist before rotation advances regardless. Without a cap the round-robin fairness guarantee breaks and one aggressive panelist can dominate a session.
3. **Call budget stops being flat** — Decision 4's `2T` becomes `T` variable. Restate as a ceiling: `T_max = base_turns + (panelists × depth_cap)`, so the budget doc still yields an RPD-reasonable maximum. Any implementation must state its `T_max`.
4. **DA interaction is benign** — DA's "last in rotation" placement (Decision 3) only *improves* under retention: chase-down turns further populate the digest before DA fires. No conflict; no redesign of Decision 2/3 required.

**Why not now (recorded so it isn't re-litigated):** round-robin unverified (would stack two moving parts before the floor is proven); breaks the flat budget being locked in Decision 4 this session; and 0.3b is already the heaviest brief on the roadmap — adding rotation control widens the diff and muddies evidence-first review. Cost of waiting is ~zero: nothing here is foreclosed by shipping fixed round-robin first.
