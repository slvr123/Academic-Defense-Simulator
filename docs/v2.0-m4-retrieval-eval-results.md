## Headline

Archetypes do retrieve different evidence, by the rule I set in advance: the median pair of archetypes shares 1 of its top-5 chunks (**Differentiated**).
That holds cleanly on DAZSMA and only partly on `sample3.pdf`, where the median pair shares 2 (**Partial**).
The real defect is somewhere else.
On `sample3.pdf`, 6 of the 8 archetypes' best match is front matter, and 4 of them land on a page that is entirely table of contents.
Every one of the six recommended-panel sessions on that document hands the candidate at least one question whose source passage is the abstract or the table of contents.
On DAZSMA, the List of Tables surfaces in two of six sessions, but only after exclusion pushes an archetype off its first choice.
The embedder sees under two thirds of the average chunk (66% on DAZSMA, 48% on sample3), because 135 of 141 chunks run past its 256-token limit.
`top_k=1` is defensible by the pre-registered rule, but only just.

## 3. Candidate fixes (proposals only, ranked)

M4 implements none of these; choosing is mine, after this debrief.

1. **Drop navigation pages from retrieval** (Check C, case study §9 item 4).
   Mark a chunk as navigation when its pages are navigation pages, using this eval's detector at ingest, and leave it out of `retrieve()`'s candidates.
   *Expected effect,* measured offline from the committed rankings (no code changed):

   | Variant | sample3 top-1 in front matter | sample3 session turns in front matter | sample3 distinct top-1 | DAZSMA session turns in front matter |
   |---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
   | Today | 6 of 8 | 9 of 18 | 4 | 2 of 18 |
   | Drop chunks lying entirely on navigation pages | 3 of 8 | 5 of 18 | 6 | 0 of 18 |
   | Drop chunks touching a navigation page | 0 of 8 | 0 of 18 | 6 | 0 of 18 |

   Max displacement stays at 2-3 either way.
   *Downside:* "touching" also drops chunk 3 on sample3, which runs from the end of the contents into the start of Chapter 1 (pages 4-6), and chunk 1, which starts on the abstract; "entirely" leaves the abstract-plus-contents chunk in.
   The detector is a heuristic: a results page whose lines mostly end in numbers could be flagged (the anicheck results page reached 33 of 90 lines, under the 50% line, so it wasn't).
   It changes ingest, so the sidecar and ingest-contract tests need a look.
2. **Embed the whole chunk, not its first 256 tokens** (Check D).
   Embed each chunk as overlapping 256-token windows and mean-pool them into one vector, keeping the chunk text the prompt sees unchanged.
   *Expected effect:* each vector describes 100% of its chunk instead of 48-66%; whether that improves relevance is unproven (September: no hit@1 gain on 8 labelled cases).
   *Downside:* about 2-3x the embedding work at ingest, on a free instance M1 sized for the current load, so the memory and time gates need re-measuring.
3. **Enrich the archetype queries** (Check A on sample3, Partial).
   For example, append the document's own title or abstract terms to each focus string, or write two or three sub-queries per archetype and take the best.
   *Expected effect:* pulls the four research-design lanes (ME, PO, SA, RC) apart on documents like sample3, and makes contents-page vocabulary less dominant.
   *Downside:* the fixed-query determinism that made this eval cheap goes away if the enrichment is generated per session, and any Gemini-generated query adds a call and a failure mode; it needs a re-run of this eval to show it helped.
4. **Sample among the top 3 instead of `top_k=1`.**
   Not licensed by Check B (43.75% ≤ 50%); listed only because it is the case study's fix for cross-session repetition, which M4 didn't measure.
   *Expected effect:* different sources across repeat sessions.
   *Downside:* costs relevance where the gap is wide (DAZSMA ME 0.098, sample3 SA 0.141), and on sample3 the top 3 often include the contents page.

My read: fix 1 is the clear first move, small and deterministic, and it removes the one defect a visitor can see on screen.
Fix 2 is worth an eval of its own before it ships.

## 4. Concepts map

- **Embeddings and cosine similarity.**
  The model turns a passage into 384 numbers, so that passages with similar meaning end up pointing in similar directions.
  Cosine similarity measures how closely two such directions agree; the vectors are normalised, so it's a dot product (`backend/embedder.py` `encode_one`; `rag/retrieval.py` `scores = chunk_matrix @ query_vec`).
- **Max sequence length and truncation.**
  The model reads at most 256 tokens, and the tokenizer silently drops everything after that.
  A 557-token chunk is embedded from its first 256 tokens only, so its vector can miss what the rest of it says (`backend/embedder.py` `MAX_SEQ_LENGTH`, `enable_truncation`; Check D).
- **Why a fixed query per archetype makes retrieval identical across sessions.**
  Each archetype's query is its `archetype_focus` string, which never changes, so its vector and its ranking of a given document never change either.
  With `top_k=1` and deterministic exclusion, the same document and panel always produce the same pages (`engine.py:382`; `prompts/panelist_prompts.py` `ARCHETYPE_CONFIG`).
- **Overlap and random baselines.**
  Overlap counts how many of two archetypes' top-5 passages are the same.
  The baseline, 25 ÷ chunks, is what two random top-5 lists would share, so an overlap means something only next to it (`evals/retrieval_checks.py` `pair_overlaps`, `random_overlap_baseline`).
- **Near-ties.**
  When the best passage beats the second by less than 0.02, the model has no real preference between them, and `top_k=1` is a coin toss dressed up as a choice.
  The rate of near-ties says whether picking only the top one is principled (`score_spread`, `near_tie_rate`).
- **Base rates.**
  A count means little without the share you'd expect by chance: 6 of 8 front-matter picks is alarming only because front matter is 8% of the chunks.
  The ratio of pick rate to base rate (9.19 on sample3) is the comparison (`front_matter_summary`).
- **Pre-registered decision rules.**
  I wrote what each result would mean before any data existed (F6), so I couldn't move the line once I saw where the numbers fell.
  That's why sample3 "Partial" and DAZSMA's exact 50% near-tie rate are reported against the rule as written (`overlap_verdict`; the runner's `summary`).
- **Why exclusion can hide collapse.**
  The engine never gives two panelists the same passage, so the screen always shows different sources even if every panelist wanted the same one.
  Displacement rank, where the passage a panelist got sits in its own ranking, shows whether it got what it wanted or a leftover (`engine.py:388` `exclude_indices`; `replay_new_topic_turns`).
