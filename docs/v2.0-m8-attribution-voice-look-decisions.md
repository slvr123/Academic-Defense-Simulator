Round 1's wording stays in the M7 doc for the record.

#### K3. Round-2 probe and verdict (pre-committed)

### Slice 2 — voice cloning

#### K4. Why
In M6, sentence-split VoiceDesign failed my by-ear check: the voice changed at the second sentence, because VoiceDesign invents a voice from its description on every call. Production whole-question clips took 6.8–14.1 s (2026-10-27). `mimo-v2.5-tts-voiceclone` copies a voice from a reference recording, so separately synthesized sentences should sound like the same person.

#### K5. The design
- **Reference clips:** one per archetype, synthesized once with today's VoiceDesign voice (v1's voice map and design strings, unchanged), 6–10 s of a fixed neutral sentence, and committed under `backend/voice_refs/`. Keep the total under 3 MB. These become each panelist's voice identity.
- **Mode switch:** `ADS_TTS_VOICE_MODE` = `design` (default, today's behaviour) or `clone`. In `clone` mode, `/tts` sends the archetype's reference clip with the text. It combines with `ADS_TTS_SPLIT_SENTENCES`. Everything else about `/tts` is unchanged: the abuse limits, validation, logging (no text) and the threadpool.
- **Measurement (pre-authorized MiMo calls):** first confirm `voiceclone` is still free with one call; if not → stop slice 2. Then, on the four questions from the 2026-10-27 live run (real lengths, not toy sentences): whole-question clone latency, and split-clone time to the first audio.
- **Clips for my ears,** in gitignored `voice-check/clone/`: for 3 archetypes, `clone-split-joined`, `clone-whole` and today's `design-whole`, all of the same question.
- **Verdict (pre-committed):** I turn on `ADS_TTS_VOICE_MODE=clone` **and** `ADS_TTS_SPLIT_SENTENCES=true` only if (a) the median time to first audio is ≤ 6 s, **and** (b) in `clone-split-joined` the same person carries on across sentences, **and** (c) the clone still sounds like the right panelist (close enough to `design-whole`). (a) is measured; (b) and (c) are my ears. Otherwise both stay off and voice speed is parked until after launch.

*Downside I accept:* each `/tts` call carries the reference clip, and 9 small WAVs live in the repo.

### Slice 3 — look and feel

#### K6. The Bench design is the source of truth
Port the visual language of `docs/design/bench/` into the React app, using its data structure and **not its mock copy**. Its panelist names, AniCheck text and "Turn 5 of 8" are placeholders; the app renders real API data.

- **Tokens:** ground `#14120F`; surfaces `#1A1714`, `#1C1915` and `#201C17`; borders `#2E2923`, `#37312A` and `#3A3125`; text `#EDE6D6`; muted text `#ABA08C`, `#9C927F` and `#8C8270`; accent amber `#E3A75B` (hover `#F0C489`); the "preparing" accent `#8AA6D6`; source-passage paper `#EFE7D6`, with ink `#2A241C` and highlight `#F0C27E`. These go in Tailwind's theme (M3 D7) and replace the current palette. The per-archetype accent colours are retired: only the active panelist is lit, in amber.
- **Type:** Newsreader (serif: questions, headings), Instrument Sans (body), IBM Plex Mono (labels, metadata), via the mockup's Google Fonts link.
- **The bench:** every panelist seated in a row, with only the speaking or active one lit (amber top bar, glow, highlighted name plate). The others are muted, and the Devil's Advocate's plate has a dashed outline. **Keep v1's PNG avatars**: inactive ones are dimmed (desaturated, lower opacity), not replaced by the mockup's silhouettes.
- **The question:** Newsreader at the mockup's size, the "Grounded in a passage from your document — revealed after you answer" line, and the turn-progress steps (Asking → Your answer → Reviewing → Handoff), driven by the real `phase`.
- **The source reveal:** the passage on a "paper" card (Reveal mockup), labelled "PDF page(s) N" (M7 J6). Inside it, **highlight the question's `grounding_reference`** when it matches the passage text (the same fuzzy match as the grounding check). If the API doesn't expose `grounding_reference` per question, add it (an additive field). No "Also retrieved" list: retrieval returns one chunk.
- **Contrast:** every text/background pair passes WCAG AA (4.5:1 body, 3:1 for 24 px+). Fix any mockup shade that fails, and list the fixes.
- **Phone width:** follow `bench-mobile.dc.html`. The 375 px check must pass, as in M3.

#### K7. Scores stay sealed until the debrief
The Bench mockup says "Answer recorded. Scores stay sealed until the debrief." That matches the project's original rule: scoring steers difficulty quietly, without surfacing mid-session. So after each answer the app shows the source reveal and the panelist's next move, **not** the scores. All scores appear in the report.
*Downside I accept:* less immediate feedback mid-defense. **This supersedes M3's per-answer score display.**

#### K8. The debrief report
- **Headline score at the top.** The backend adds `overall_raw` to the report: the mean of the three dimension averages (clarity, depth, grounding), computed in Python. The frontend rescales it with `scoreDisplay` (v1.2.1's formula), like every other score. Below it, the three dimension averages.
- **One card per question:** the question, my answer, the source passage (paper card, with the highlight), the gap the panelist found (`primary_gap`) and the suggested stronger answer. Collapsed by default except the first. The narrative and the full transcript stay.
- `AnswerScore` and the rubric are untouched (standing rule).

#### K9. Motion: CSS only, only where it shows state
No Framer Motion and no new dependency: CSS transitions and keyframes only.
- **Allowed:** the bench lighting moving to the next panelist (handoff), a subtle pulse while a panelist is speaking, a "reviewing" shimmer while an answer is scored, and the source reveal sliding in.
- **Not allowed:** anything decorative.
- Every animation is disabled under `prefers-reduced-motion`, and the typewriter rule (M3 D9) stays.

*Downside I accept:* less springy than Framer Motion. That's acceptable for a defense, which shouldn't feel like a game intro.
