## Decision 1 — Avatar art is ground truth; the gender metadata bends (LOCKED)

The Methodology Expert renders a female avatar and speaks with a male voice.
Whatever the voice map's gender entry says, **the art is what the user sees**, so
the art wins and the metadata is corrected to match it. I am not commissioning or
swapping avatar art to satisfy a map entry.

This is stated as a general principle, not a one-off, because it will recur every
time art and metadata drift: **art is the observable, the map is the description,
and a description that disagrees with the observable is the thing that's wrong.**

*Provenance: I reported the symptom; Claude proposed art-as-ground-truth as a
standing principle rather than a one-off fix; I locked it.*

**In plain terms:** the picture is right, the label is wrong, fix the label.

## Decision 2 — Diagnose before editing the map, with a pre-committed branch rule (LOCKED)

There are at least two causes that produce the identical symptom, and they have
different fixes and different blast radii. Editing the map before knowing which
one is live risks papering over a latent bug that affects other archetypes on
other machines.

**Branch A — metadata mismatch.** The map's gender entry for
`methodology_expert` says male; the avatar is female. → Correct the gender entry,
then apply Decision 3.

**Branch B — resolution fallback.** The gender entry already says female, but the
`voice_prefs` list for `methodology_expert` names voices that do not resolve on
this browser, so resolution fell through to a male default. → Fix the prefs list
so the first entries are voices verified to exist in the Windows/Edge and
Windows/Chrome inventories. **Report which other archetypes have unresolvable
names at the head of their prefs lists** — the same latent bug will be sitting on
them, and the handoff already records that the prefs lists name Apple voices
never observed to resolve on a real machine.

**Branch C — both.** Apply both fixes above, in that order.

**Branch D — none of the above.** The symptom is real but is produced by
something neither A nor B describes (e.g. the gender entry is correct, prefs
resolve correctly, and the male voice is arriving from somewhere else entirely).
→ **Stop. Report the resolution trace and bring it back to design.** Do not
invent a fix inside the Code session.

This rule enumerates a halt branch on purpose — codified after the sleep-fix
rule failure where a malformed pre-committed rule contained a precondition
swallowed by its own override.

*Provenance: Claude proposed the A/B split and the halt branch; I locked it and
required the "other archetypes affected" report under branch B.*

**In plain terms:** find out *why* the voice is wrong before changing anything,
because one of the two possible causes means several other panelists are quietly
broken too. If it's neither cause, stop and ask me.

## Decision 3 — Reassign only if a male voice becomes wholly unused (LOCKED)

Flipping `methodology_expert` to female removes one male from the
male-voice contest. **What that buys depends on a fact I do not currently have:**
the v1.2 handoff names only two reuse pairs (`ethics`+`technical` on Microsoft
David, `results`+`industry` on Microsoft Mark) against three male voices, which
does not account for a male-voiced Methodology Expert. So either Methodology is a
third member of an existing group, or the handoff's reuse record is incomplete.
Either way, that discrepancy is itself information this doc needs.

**Rule:**

- If, after the flip, a male voice is left with **zero** archetypes on it →
  reassign one member of the larger remaining reuse group onto the freed voice.
  This retires half of the open DoD 5 listening item for free, because the pair
  in question stops being a pitch-only separation.
- If no male voice is left wholly unused (i.e. Methodology was a third member of
  an existing group) → **change nothing else.** Flip the gender, take the best
  available female voice, and record explicitly that the reuse pairs are
  unchanged and DoD 5 remains open in full.

**Do not re-permute the map.** The handoff is explicit that map-layer
re-permutation is not where the fix lives.

**New-collision guard:** the flip moves Methodology into the female pool, which
may now seat more female archetypes in a default panel than there are distinct
female voices. The strict default-panel test exists to catch exactly this. **If
it fails, that is Branch D — halt and bring it back to design. Do not weaken the
test in this session.** The handoff records the two legitimate ways to weaken it;
neither is authorized here.

*Provenance: Claude proposed the freed-voice reassignment and flagged the
handoff/reality discrepancy; I locked the conditional form and the
do-not-weaken-the-test constraint.*

**In plain terms:** if fixing Methodology leaves a male voice completely unused,
grab it to un-double one of the doubled-up pairs. If it doesn't, just fix
Methodology and leave everything else alone. If the test that guards this goes
red, stop — don't loosen the test to get green.

## Decision 4 — Score display: presentation layer only, `(x / 5) × 100` (LOCKED)

**`AnswerScore` is untouched.** `clarity`, `depth`, `grounding` stay
`ge=1, le=5`. `SCORING_SYSTEM_PROMPT` is untouched. This is standing constraint 3:
changing the rubric or its wording invalidates every score-related eval finding in
the case study, and I am not paying that bill for a display change.

**Mapping: `(x / 5) × 100`.** So 1→20, 3→60, 4→80, 5→100.

I considered `((x − 1) / 4) × 100`, which has a true zero and maps 3→50. Rejected:
my users read 75 as passing, so a mid-scale answer displayed as 50% reads as a
failing grade and distorts the feedback more than a 20% floor does.

**The 20% floor is stated, not hidden.** A caption renders under the score block:

> Panel ratings are a 1–5 judgment rescaled to a 100-point display, not a
> percentage of correct answers. The scale floor is 20.

An ordinal LLM judgment dressed as a percentage manufactures precision it does not
have. One caption line is the whole cost of keeping that honest, and this project's
record on that is worth more than the caption costs.

**Rounding:** round half up to the nearest integer. Averages are computed on the
raw 1–5 values and rescaled at the end — never rescale then average.

**What stays on 1–5:** the export JSON (it is the record), dev-view, all
persisted session data, and every test fixture. Only rendered report surfaces
change.

*Provenance: I asked for 1–100; Claude proposed the two candidate mappings, the
floor-honesty caption, and rescale-after-averaging; I locked `(x / 5) × 100`.*

**In plain terms:** the numbers the AI produces don't change at all — we just show
them on a 100-point scale in the report, with one line under it saying what that
scale actually is. Everything saved to disk keeps the original 1–5.

## Decision 5 — The narrative call gets rescaled numbers, not a rewritten prompt (LOCKED)

`REPORT_NARRATIVE_PROMPT` receives the computed `DefenseReport` serialized to
JSON and is instructed to summarize *from the provided data only*. If the UI shows
68 and the narrative prose says "3.4 out of 5", the report contradicts itself.

**Fix: rescale the numbers in the payload passed to the narrative call. Do not
edit the template wording.** The template asks the model to summarize what it is
given; giving it 68 instead of 3.4 requires no wording change and does not move
`PROMPT_VERSION`.

**Precondition, and it is a halt condition.** If the template text names the scale
anywhere — "1–5", "out of 5", "on a five-point scale" — then this cannot be done
without a wording change, which triggers the Day 4 re-verification rule.
**In that case: stop, report the exact line, and bring it back to design.** Do not
edit the template.

Same check applies to `ANSWER_SUGGESTIONS_PROMPT_VERSION` and
`ANALYTICS_PROMPT_VERSION` surfaces: if either template names the scale, report it
and halt on that surface rather than editing.

*Provenance: Claude caught the narrative/UI contradiction and proposed the
payload-rescale route plus the halt condition; I locked it.*

**In plain terms:** the write-up model only ever sees the numbers we hand it, so
we hand it the new numbers and change nothing about its instructions. If its
instructions happen to mention "out of 5", that's a bigger job than this slice and
we stop.

## Decision 6 — Question humanization is NOT in this slice (RECORDED, deferred)

I wanted to make questions sound more human — filler words, emotional
undertones, varied sentence length, colloquialisms, deliberate word substitution,
subtle imperfections. **Deferred, not rejected.** Recorded here so it is not
re-derived, in the same shape as the Day 4 OpenRouter/Groq rejection and the v1.2
voice-options table.

| Element | Verdict | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Contractions, varied sentence length | **Keep for later slice** | Cheap, low grounding risk, genuinely improves realism. |
| Short lead-in clause before the question ("You mention X on page 12 — I want to press on that.") | **Keep for later slice** | Reads as a real panelist without touching the question's precision. |
| Hedges ("it seems", "perhaps") on the framing clause | **Keep, narrowly** | Fine on the lead-in; **not** on the question itself — a hedged question is easier to dodge, and dodging is the thing this app trains against. |
| Filler / disfluency tokens ("um", "uh") | **Rejected** | Reads as texture on the page, sounds like a bug through TTS. v1.2 made this actively worse than it was a week ago. |
| Humor, sarcasm, personality | **Rejected** | Real panels are dry. A quipping panelist is less realistic, not more. |
| Colloquialisms, idioms, region-specific expressions | **Rejected** | Collides with the archetype lane wording locked in v1.1a. |
| "Deliberately unexpected word alternatives" | **Rejected — the dangerous one** | Pushes the model to paraphrase away from source vocabulary. `grounding_reference` is matched exact-then-fuzzy against the retrieved chunk, and `is_grounded()` already has a documented systematic false-negative mode on assembled references. This instruction aims directly at it. |
| "Personal opinions" | **Rejected** | Collides with lane constraints. |
| "Subtle imperfections, minor redundancies" | **Rejected** | Same TTS problem, plus it degrades question precision for no measurable gain. |

**Why it's deferred rather than done now:** every keeper above is a
prompt-template edit, which fires the Day 4 rule — `PROMPT_VERSION` bumps and the
archetype lane evidence, `difficulty_ok` rows, and grounding rate all go
unverified until re-probed. That is a probe session, not a quick fix, and the
retrieval eval has already been queued behind presentation work for an entire
slice.

**Stopping rule for when it does land:** framing clause + contractions +
sentence-length variance, no disfluency tokens, no humor, no vocabulary
substitution. One probe run against DAZSMA checking the grounding rate has not
moved. Closed either way on that one run.

*Provenance: I proposed the humanization block (sourced from a general
AI-detector-evasion prompt); Claude pushed back on the specific elements and
priced the re-verification; I locked the split and the deferral.*

**In plain terms:** making the panelists sound human is worth doing, but the
version I pasted was written to fool AI detectors, not to sound like a real
examiner — and half of it would break the grounding check. The good parts are
written down with a hard stopping rule, and they wait until after the retrieval
eval.
